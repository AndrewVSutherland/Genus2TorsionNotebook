## 1. Where [6,12] stands and what "E8" is

The [6,12] route (`notes/contact6_m612_route.md` and successors) reduces the existence of a
genus-2 curve with `J(C)(Q)_tors ⊇ Z/6 × Z/12` on the contact-6 chart to rational points of an
explicit **genus-4 curve**

```
E8 :  Q8(x,y) = y^8 + A(x) y^4 + B(x) y^2 + C(x) = 0
      A = 216x^4 + 72x^3 - 24x^2
      B = -1296x^6 - 1728x^5 - 432x^4 + 64x^3
      C = -3888x^8 - 2592x^7 + 432x^6 + 288x^5 - 48x^4
```

(`x = e`, `y = e·w`; source `data/contact6_m612_E8_mp8Q.txt`). `E8` carries the involution
`iota : y -> -y` with quotient the genus-2 curve `E4` (substitute `Z = y^2/x`). Riemann–Hurwitz
`2·4-2 = 2(2·2-2) + r` forces `r = 2`: **iota has exactly two fixed points**, and they are the
two rational places over `x = 0` (ramification indices 2 and 6 in `E8 -> P^1_x`, hence each is
individually iota-stable). Call them `b1, b2`; they are the *boundary* points and correspond to
degenerate (non-usable) fibres.

`E8(Q) = {b1,b2}` would **close [6,12] on this route** (negatively): no curve of the family
carries the required torsion.

The Prym `Pr := (1-iota) J(E8)` is an abelian surface, and

```
alpha : E8 -> Pr,   alpha(P) = [P - iota P]
```

satisfies `alpha(P) = 0  <=>  P in {b1,b2}` (otherwise `P - iota P = div(h)` would give a
degree-1 map from a genus-4 curve to `P^1`). So

> **`E8(Q) = {b1,b2}`  ⟺  `alpha(P) = 0` for every rational `P`.**

`rank Pr(Q) = 1 < 2 = dim Pr`, which is what makes both Chabauty and an MW sieve possible.

## 3. NEW, and exact: `Lambda` is cyclic over **Q**

`code/claude_ov_m612ap_relq.m`, log `results/claude_ov_m612ap_relq.log`. This works on the
genus-4 function field of `E8` **over Q**, not mod `q`, and asks `IsPrincipal` of degree-0
divisors:

| divisor | `IsPrincipal` |
|---|---|
| `D1  = P_{g1} - P_{g1(-y)}` | **true** |
| `D23 = (P_{g2}-P_{g2(-y)}) - (P_{g3}-P_{g3(-y)})` | **true** |
| `D2  = P_{g2} - P_{g2(-y)}` | **false** |

Magma returns the generating functions explicitly (they are in the log; the `D23` one is a
degree-12 expression in `X` and `yy`). Therefore, **exactly and unconditionally**,

> **`W1 = 0`, `W3 = W2`, `W2 != 0` in `Pr(Q)`, so `Lambda = <W1,W2,W3> = <W>` is cyclic
> with the single explicit generator `W := W2` (`x0 = -2/5`).**

`W` has infinite order: rational torsion divides 9 (§5) but `ord(W mod 13) = 5`.
Since `rank Pr(Q) = 1`, `<W>` has finite index in `Pr(Q)/tors`, and the sieve becomes a
genuine **one-generator** Mordell–Weil sieve. This replaces the mod-`q` observation
"`ord(W1 mod q) = 1` and `W3 = W2` at every prime" with a proof.

## 4. The one inherited hypothesis, cross-checked: `Pr ~ J(D)`

Everything rests on `rank Pr(Q) = 1`, which comes from `Pr ~ J(D)`. That identification came
out of the bigonal construction and had never been checked independently.
`code/claude_ov_m612ap_prymid.m` compares **full L-polynomials** at each good prime:

```
L_{E8}(T)  =?  L_{E4}(T) · L_D(T)          (Frobenius on Pr = Frobenius on J(D))
```

Result: **`MATCH true` at every prime tested** (see `results/prymid/`, and §5's table for the
prime list). Sample, `q = 13`:

```
L8 = [1, 6, 44, 204, 789, 2652, 7436, 13182, 28561]
L4 = [1, 4, 9, 52, 169]        LD = [1, 2, 27, 26, 169]      L8 = L4·LD
#J(E8)(F_13) = 52875 = 235 · 225 = #J(E4)(F_13) · #J(D)(F_13)
```

which also reproduces the sieve's independent `JORDER/JE4ORD/PRYMORD` numbers. This is the
standard practical isogeny certificate (equality of all local L-factors forces isogeny by
Faltings/Tate); it is *not* a proof, because only finitely many primes were checked and no
effective Faltings bound was computed.

Consequence used below: `J(D)(Q)_tors = 0`, so `Pr(Q)` is very likely torsion free — but an
isogeny need not preserve torsion, so the sieve is reported in a torsion-robust variant too.

## 5. The Abel–Prym Mordell–Weil sieve — what was actually run

**Worker** `code/claude_ov_m612ap_sieve3.m` (rewrite of `sieve2.m`), one forked child per
prime, one global Magma slot. Per prime `q` it computes:

1. good reduction (`IsIrreducible(Q8)`, `Genus = 4`), the two boundary places over `x=0` with
   ramification `(2,6)`;
2. the **iota-equivariant key** for degree-1 places — position data (`x`-value or `inf`, and
   the ramification index over the `x`-line) is iota-invariant, `y` (resp. `y/x` at infinity)
   is anti-invariant, and the uniformiser is invariant, so negating the expansion
   coefficients of `y` in an invariant uniformiser yields exactly the key of `iota(R)`. This
   resolves the node-collision that a naive `(x,y)` key hits. Asserted: the key is injective,
   `iota` is an involution on the keys, it fixes exactly **2** degree-1 places (Riemann–Hurwitz)
   and those are the two boundary places;
3. `J = J(E8)(F_q) = TorsionSubgroup(ClassGroup(F))`;
4. `W_i mod q`, by reducing the **divisors** (fibres over `x0` are étale mod `q`);
5. for every non-fixed degree-1 place `R`, the class `c_R = [R - iota R]` and its discrete log
   in `<W mod q>`; also the discrete logs of `3·c_R` and `9·c_R`;
6. `ell`-divisibility of `W mod q` for every `ell <= 200` dividing `#J`, which is the
   saturation test.

Three fixes over `sieve2`: per-**generator** skip (recovers `q = 7,11,19` — the ramified primes
are `gen1: 2,3,5`, `gen2: 2,3,5,19`, `gen3: 2,3,5,7,11`, discriminants factored in PARI);
the `RES3`/`RES9` lines (torsion robustness, below); and the parse-error fix.

**Regression** (`code/claude_ov_m612ap_regress.py`): on the **20 primes computed by both**
workers, all twelve tagged fields (`JORDER JINV WORDERS SORDER NONFIXED INS RES2 MINMULT
JE4ORD PRYMORD DIVISIBLE TESTED`) are identical. The rewrite changed nothing except what it
was meant to change.

**Combination** `code/claude_ov_m612ap_combine2.py` — per prime power `ell^a | ord(W mod q)`
it intersects the admissible residues over all contributing primes, then reports for each `ell`
the largest `ell^b` dividing *every* surviving residue. The per-`ell^b` divisibility conclusion
is *optimal* for the data (the projection of the admissible set of one prime onto one prime
power is exact, and intersecting projections across primes is exactly the right operation). The
"surviving classes" count is only an **upper bound**: it multiplies the per-`ell` counts and so
ignores the correlation, within a single prime `q`, between the different prime-power parts of
`ord(W mod q)`. A full CRT would only ever make that count smaller.

### The three variants and why there are three

If `P` is rational then `alpha(P) = d·W + t` with `t ∈ Pr(Q)_tors`, and mod `q` this must equal
`c_R` for `R = P mod q`. So the admissible `d` at `q` are:

* **V0** `{d : c_R = d·W mod q}` — valid **iff `t = 0`**;
* **V3** `{d : 3d·W = 3c_R mod q}` — valid iff `3t = 0`;
* **V9** `{d : 9d·W = 9c_R mod q}` — valid iff `9t = 0`.

Our own data pins the torsion without appeal to `J(D)`: `gcd_q #J(E8)(F_q) = 9` bounds
`#J(E8)(Q)_tors`, and the 3-primary part of `J(E8)(F_7)` is `(Z/3)^2`, of **exponent 3**, into
which the rational torsion injects. Hence `3t = 0` always and **V3 is unconditional** (given
saturation). V0 is the sharp variant, valid when `Pr(Q)_tors = 0`. `J(D)(Q)_tors = 0` (§4) makes
that very likely but does **not** imply it: torsion is not an isogeny invariant, and the isogeny
`Pr -> J(D)` has unknown degree. So V0 is reported as conditional and V3 as the honest number.

## 6. What the bound means, and why it does not close [6,12]

The sieve output is a **congruence**, `d ≡ 0 (mod M)`. It can never give `d = 0`: for that one
needs a height bound `|d| < M`, i.e. the Chabauty/Coleman half of the argument, or an explicit
canonical-height bound on `Pr(Q) = <W>` together with a bound on `ĥ(alpha(P))` for `P ∈ E8(Q)`.
Neither exists in this repository.

What is therefore proved (modulo saturation, §5) is:

> Every rational point `P` of `E8` other than `b1, b2` has `alpha(P) = d·W` with `M | d`,
> `d != 0`; hence `ĥ(alpha(P)) = d^2 ĥ(W) >= M^2 ĥ(W)`.

With `ĥ(W) = ĥ(g0) = 4.3676…` (§4a; the equality is the `W ↔ ±g0` identification, so this
figure is exactly as conditional as that is):

| variant | `M` | `ĥ(alpha(P)) >= M^2 · ĥ(W)` |
|---|---|---|
| V3 (unconditional in the torsion) | 11 200 | `5.48 × 10^8` |
| V0 (needs `Pr(Q)_tors = 0`) | 3 729 600 | `6.08 × 10^13` |

Those are preposterous heights for a curve of this size — which is why the honest summary is
"morally closed, not closed".

Together with the (measured) fact that no rational point exists with `x`-height `<= 10 000`
(121 589 943 values of `x`, §2), this is strong evidence for `E8(Q) = {b1,b2}` and hence for
[6,12] being empty on this route — but it is evidence, not a theorem.

**(D2) Fabricated precision — lines 20, 167–171 against the log.**

Sixteen digits of the annihilator are invented. Every zero the sweep reports then carries a

**(D3) Tuitman's `inf` flag is printed and then discarded — lines 204–212, 220–245.**

In an infinity disk Tuitman's `x` is the coordinate on the *other* chart, so testing
`Q8(e0, y)` tests the wrong fibre; and the `Valuation(x0) lt 0` branch drops such zeros
outright. Both are **false negatives**, which is the fatal direction for a Chabauty argument —
it must account for *every* zero.
*Fix:* carry `<P`x, P`inf>` through and dispatch the exact fibre test on the flag.

**(D5) An uncertified assumption inside the pairing** — lines 155–159 assume the two boundary

goes through the bigonal correspondence. The intrinsic classes of §2–§3 exist precisely to
avoid this; a rebuilt Coleman driver should pair against `W` directly.

**(D6) — and this is the one that matters most — fixing D1–D3 is not enough.**
The defects above are coercion/bookkeeping bugs; the *arithmetic* problem is that the run only
ever **achieved** 4 significant 37-adic digits on the annihilator coefficients
(`omega_ann coefficients: 473735 + O(37^4)`), with `N := 20` as the working precision. Tuitman's
`coleman_integrals_on_basis` reported `prec 5` on **every** one of the six integrals, so the
loss is intrinsic to the run's parameters, not to the post-processing. Four digits cannot
certify a root count in a residue disk: `zeros_on_disk` needs the valuations of the local series
coefficients to be *known*, and beyond digit 4 they are noise. Therefore a precision-safe driver
must **also raise `N`** substantially (and re-tune `e`/`ee`), which re-costs the whole sweep —
the README's "12–24 h at `p = 37`" is an underestimate for a run that would actually prove
anything. Any rebuild should first do a one-disk calibration: raise `N` until
`coleman_integrals_on_basis` returns ≥ ~12 correct digits, measure the cost of that single disk,
and only then commit to 42 of them.

so `git clone https://github.com/jtuitman/Coleman` would work, but per D6 the resulting
computation is a multi-day job at a precision that has never been costed, which does not fit
this session and should not be started blind.

## 8. What I did NOT check

* **Saturation of `<W>` in `Pr(Q)` — the one real gap.** The sieve conclusion assumes
  `Pr(Q) = <W> + tors`. Three independent things now support it, none of them a proof of it:
  (i) the local test — `ell` can divide the index only if `W` is `ell`-divisible in
  `J(E8)(F_q)` for **every** `q` with `ell | #J(E8)(F_q)`; the `ell` that survive that test are
  listed in §5 and are all `>= 23`; (ii) `g0` provably generates `J(D)(Q)` (§4a);
  (iii) `ord(W mod q) = ord(g0 mod q)` at 25/25 primes (§4b). What is missing is the link
  between (ii)+(iii) and `W` itself, i.e. a **certified** isogeny `Pr -> J(D)` under which
  `W ↦ ±g0`. The bigonal transport that would supply it is exactly the thing this lane's
  intrinsic classes were built to avoid relying on, and it is still uncertified.
* **The isogeny `Pr ~ J(D)`** is supported by matching *full* L-polynomials at 19 primes and
  matching point counts `#Pr(F_q) = #J(D)(F_q)` at 25 primes — not proved. No effective
  Faltings/Serre bound was computed, and no explicit isogeny was exhibited.
* **`rank Pr(Q) = 1`** rests on `RankBounds(J(D)) = 1..1` plus that isogeny. I *did* re-run
  `code/contact6_m612_prym_rank_verifier.m` this session (`results/claude_ov_m612ap_prymrank_rerun.log`:
  `RankBounds = 1..1`, `torsion invariants = []`, generator `(x^2+2x+4, 5x+5)`), but the rank

* **No Coleman/Chabauty computation was run** (§7): diagnosis only.
* **The mod-`q` sieve only sees degree-1 places.** That is correct (a rational point reduces to
  a degree-1 place) but means the sieve says nothing about rational points of `E8` in the bad
  fibres it skips, i.e. at primes of bad reduction — those are excluded by the good-reduction

* **Curve-level torsion.** Nothing in this lane certifies any *curve* with `[6,12]`; the lane is
