# Plan: realize [2,2,6,6] and [12,12] on split Jacobians via HLP-§3.7-type conditions

*Written 2026-08-13, for execution in a fresh session.  Both targets have
order 144 and would set a new split-torsion order record (current maximum:
128 = [2,2,4,8], the maximum of HLP's Table 1).  Both are product groups
([2,6]×[2,6] and [12]×[12]) absent from HLP's Table 1 and from the 6.2M-curve
extended database.*

## 0. Background established on 2026-08-12/13 (do not re-derive)

- **Image arithmetic.** For a (2,2)-gluing J ~ (E×F)/Γ, Γ = graph of a
  Galois anti-isometry ψ: E[2]→F[2], the odd part of J(Q)_tors is exactly
  E(Q)_odd × F(Q)_odd, and the image of E(Q)×F(Q) has 2-part
  (T1[2^∞]×T2[2^∞])/(Γ ∩ rational part).  Measured generic outcomes:
  [2,6]×[2,6] → **[6,6]** (order 36); [12]×[12] → **[6,12]** (order 72).
  So [2,2,6,6] needs gains of index 4 over the [2,6]² image and [12,12]
  needs index 2 over the [12]² image.  Blind gains are rare
  (glue2/gainhunt logs: 3 gains in ~9000 exact computations, none reaching
  order 144); HLP §3.7 (Prop. 12) is the tool that FORCES them.
- **HLP machinery in hand** (arXiv math/9809210, full text extracted in the
  2026-08-12 session): universal curves E^t_{2,6}, E^u_{2,8} rebuilt from
  Table 5 x-coordinates in `product/code/lane_hlp37.m` (runtime-verified);
  instantiating their §3.7 square conditions (5)/(6) produced the first
  exact [2,2,24] and [2,2,4,8] witnesses (verified,
  `product/logs/verify_witnesses2.log`).  The same mechanics are the
  template for everything below.
- **Funnel infrastructure**: `product/code/split_lab.m` (run lanes from
  `product/code/`).  [2,2,6,6] and [12,12] are NOT in KNOWN, so a hit
  prints `HIT ... invs=[ 2, 2, 6, 6 ]` / `[ 12, 12 ]` directly.
  `Genus2Elliptic2(E,F)` tries all six root-identifications ψ — always
  funnel every returned model.  House limits: ≤3 concurrent Magma jobs.

## 1. Target A: [2,2,6,6] = second 2-torsion gain on E^t_{2,6} × E^u_{2,6}

**Structure.** [2,2,6,6] has odd part [3,3] (automatic for any
[2,6]×[2,6]-gluing) and 2-part (Z/2)^4 = FULL rational J[2].  For a
class-(a) gluing the sextic is a product of three rational quadratics
(2-rank 2 = the image); 2-rank 4 ⟺ all three quadratics split ⟺ the glued
sextic has six rational Weierstrass points.  Equivalently, in Prop.-12
language: two independent halves of graph classes descend.

**HLP already did the first half.**  Their system (6) (§3.7, p. 18–19):
with ι(T1) = (2(t−3)(t+3)(t−5), (t+3)(t−5), 2(t−3)) for E^t_{2,6}
(Table 5 order: T1, T2, T3), ψ = identity, a half of (T1,T1′) descends iff

    2(t−3) = 2(u−3) y²   and   (t+3)(t−5) = (u+3)(u−5) z²      (6)

(third component automatic).  Substituting t from the first equation gives
((u−3)y²+6)((u−3)y²−2) = (u+3)(u−5)z², a genus-1 fibration over Q(u) with
origin (y,z)=(1,1) and the point (y,z)=(−1,1) of infinite order — a
positive-rank elliptic surface whose points give their [2,6,6]-containing
family (order 72; the DB curve 132300.eo2 and this repo's contact-6 bank are
exact-[2,2,6,6]-adjacent... note: exact [2,6,6] witnesses exist; none is
[2,2,6,6]).

**The task: impose a SECOND independent gain simultaneously.**
Steps for the new session:

1. Compute ι(T2) and ι(T3) for E^t_{2,6} symbolically (2 minutes in Magma:
   for y² = (x−e1)(x−e2)(x−e3) with the Table-5 e_i(t) already coded in
   `lane_hlp37.m`, ι(e_i) has components (e_i−e_j)(e_i−e_k) in slot i and
   (e_i−e_j) in slot j — clear denominators to polynomials in t and reduce
   mod squares).  Sanity gate: re-derive ι(T1) and match HLP's printed
   value EXACTLY before proceeding.
2. Write the descent condition for a half of (T2,T2′) (and of (T3,T3′)):
   two square-conditions each, of the same shape as (6), with the T2/T3
   component polynomials.  Also write the mixed-ψ variants (ψ permuting
   T-labels as in their [2,2,24] derivation) — six ψ's, each with three
   candidate classes; enumerate mechanically, dedupe.
3. **Fast numeric attack first** (this found (t,u)=(1/6, 4/17) for
   [2,2,4,8] at height 20): sweep (t,u) of height ≤ 60 testing the
   conjunction of ALL FOUR square conditions — with each condition
   "A(t) ≡ B(u) mod squares" tested as IsSquare(A(t)·B(u)) after guarding
   A(t)B(u) ≠ 0 —

       IsSquare(A_{T1,1}(t)·B_{T1,1}(u)) and IsSquare(A_{T1,2}(t)·B_{T1,2}(u))
       and IsSquare(A_{T2,1}(t)·B_{T2,1}(u)) and IsSquare(A_{T2,2}(t)·B_{T2,2}(u)),

   i.e. both conditions of the (T1,T1′) system AND both of the (T2,T2′)
   system simultaneously (two gains; a point passing only the first pair is
   just the known [2,6,6] family and must NOT reach the gluing stage).
   Pure rational arithmetic, ~10⁶ pairs/sec; no curves built until a pair
   passes all four.  Every survivor: glue with Genus2Elliptic2, funnel
   every model.  Expect exact [2,2,6,6] on survivors (or [2,6,6] if a gain
   degenerates — iterate over the ψ/class variants from step 2).
4. **Structured attack** if the sweep is dry: parametrize the (6)-surface
   by multiples of the infinite-order section (HLP's (y,z)=(−1,1)):
   n·(−1,1) for n ≤ ~12 gives a sequence of (t,u)-points on the one-gain
   locus with heights growing; test the second-gain conditions at each.
   Also fiber the second condition over the first: for each rational u on
   the base, the (6)-fiber has known points; the second condition cuts a
   double cover of that fiber = another genus-≤? curve — 2-descent or a
   point search on the double cover for small u.
5. Verify any hit with the full checklist (exact TorsionSubgroup on the
   integral model, L-poly reducibility, elliptic factors via Richelot
   SetCart, record in product/data/new_split_witnesses.txt + a note).

**Sanity anchors for step 2's algebra**: the condition system for
(T1,T1′) must reproduce (6) verbatim; the [2,2,24]-machinery
(`lane_hlp37.m`) must re-emerge when E^u_{2,6} is swapped for E^u_{2,8}
with ψ = (1 3 2).

**Watch-outs.** (i) The three quadratics' split-discriminant classes
multiply to a fixed class, so at most two of the three (T_i,T_i′)-systems
are independent — if two hold, check whether the third is then automatic
(if so, only ONE extra system beyond (6) is needed: 4 square conditions
total, not 6). (ii) Forcing all six Weierstrass points rational may
collide with the HLP nondegeneracy condition (α1 b32 + ... ≠ 0) on a
component — if every solution degenerates, switch ψ. (iii) [2,2,6,6]
needs no new local theory: (Z/2)^4 × (Z/3)² is isotropy-legal and
(C2×C6)² is a maximal Mazur product.

## 2. Target B: [12,12] = halving the order-6 generator on F^t_{12} × F^u_{12}

**Structure.**  The [12]×[12]-image is (Z12×Z12)/⟨(6g1,6g2)⟩ ≅ [6,12],
generated by u = image of (g1,g2) (order 6) and v = image of (0,g2)
(order 12).  [12,12] requires a rational c with 2c = u (up to ⟨v⟩), i.e. a
rational half of the (g1,g2)-class — a genuine 24-division descent
condition, NOT obtainable from 2-torsion shifts:

**Collapse lemma (derived 2026-08-13; include to avoid re-derivation).**
For class-(b) gluings (one rational 2-torsion point each, conjugate pair
T_c, T_c′ over the SAME quadratic field on both sides — matched by the
gluing), every candidate "half of (T,0)" of the form
(3g1 + ε, 2-torsion partner) has its Galois coboundary in Γ, but the coset
always collapses into the image after adding the graph element
(T_c, ψT_c): no rank gain and no new order-4 point arises this way.  This
is why thousands of [12]×[12]-gluings in the 2026-08-12 logs all gave
exactly [6,12].  The gain must come from halving (g1,g2) itself.

**The condition.**  A half c = (P,Q), 2(P,Q) ∈ (g1,g2)+Γ, is rational in
A = (E×F)/Γ iff the images of g1 and g2 under the 2-descent maps match
through ψ̃, exactly as in Prop. 12 but applied to the max-torsion points
instead of 2-torsion points:

    δ_E(g1) ≡ δ_F(g2)  in  (A_E ⊗ ...)*/squares,

where δ is the classical x−e_i descent map and the étale algebra is
Q × Q(√D) (class (b)).  Concretely: ONE rational square condition
(from the rational-root slot: x(g1) − e_rat(t) ≡ x(g2) − e_rat(u) mod
squares) and ONE norm/square condition in Q(√D) (conjugate-root slots),
which unpacks to two rational conditions via the norm form.  Everything is
computable because x(g1) is explicit:

1. Build F^t_{12} from the verified Kubert X1(12) parametrization
   (`product/code/lane_x1fam.m`, CANDS[12] — variant already
   runtime-verified): E(b(t), c(t)) in Tate normal form with g1 = (0,0) of
   order 12.  Translate x so the rational 2-torsion point 6·(0,0) sits
   where convenient; compute e_rat(t), the conjugate quadratic factor, and
   D(t) = its discriminant ≡ Δ12(t) mod squares.  (HLP's Tables 6–8 have
   pre-massaged F^t models — y² = x(x²+Ax+B) with torsion-point
   coordinates in Table 7 — but the pdftotext extraction mangled them;
   re-deriving from Kubert is 20 lines of Magma and self-verifying.)
2. The gluing exists iff Δ12(t) ≡ Δ12(u) mod squares (2-torsion fields
   match).  This is HLP §3.5's Δ-matching: treat Δ12(t)y² = Δ12(u) as a
   conic/genus-1 surface over Q(t) with the point (u,y) = (t,1); (t,−1)
   etc. give a positive-rank section, hence a 2-parameter supply of glued
   [12]×[12]-pairs (this is their [6,12] "prES" entry).  Parametrize it.
3. On that supply, impose the halving condition from the δ-match above
   (derive it symbolically once; reduce each slot mod squares).  Note the
   choice of ψ on the conjugate pair (two isos) and the four translates
   (g1+E[2]-elements) give a small enumeration of condition variants —
   write them all; at most 2 rational square conditions each after norm
   expansion.
4. **Numeric attack**: sweep the Δ-matched family (both the section
   multiples and a raw (t,u,y)-sweep of Δ12(t)y² = Δ12(u) at height ≤ 60,
   which is again pure IsSquare arithmetic) testing the halving
   conditions; glue survivors, funnel.  IMPORTANT check on any hit: the
   half may enlarge [6,12] to [12,12] or to [2,6,12] depending on which
   coset c lands in — both are new groups (order 144); either is a win.
   Compute the exact group and record which.
5. Validation gates: (a) on a random Δ-matched pair NOT satisfying the
   halving condition, the funnel must return [6,12] (negative control —
   plenty in `product/logs/x1fam.log`, e.g. the t=1/6,-1/6 and 1/7,-2
   pairs); (b) the δ-machinery specialized to 2-torsion points must
   reproduce Prop. 12/system (6).

**Watch-outs.** (i) x(g1)−e_i can vanish identically for a bad model
translation — pick the translation with g1 ∉ 2E. (ii) The descent-map
convention, stated in full so no external note is needed: for
J = Jac(y² = f(x)) with f = c·∏(x−e_i) and a class with monic Mumford
representative U of degree d ∈ {1,2}, the slot of root e_i is
δ_i = (−1)^d · U(e_i) ∈ Q*/(Q*)² whenever U(e_i) ≠ 0; when U(e_i) = 0
(the divisor meets the Weierstrass point over e_i), replace the vanishing
factor by f′(e_i): for d = 2 with U-roots {e_i, r₂} the slot is
f′(e_i)·(−1)·(e_i−r₂), and for U = x−e_i it is f′(e_i).  For an elliptic
factor y² = (x−e1)(x−e2)(x−e3) and P ∉ E[2] this is
δ(P) = (x(P)−e1, x(P)−e2, x(P)−e3), with the i-th slot replaced by
f′(e_i) at a 2-torsion point (e_i, 0); the slots multiply to a square —
use that as a checksum.  The committed, cross-validated implementation is
`delta_of_U` in `code/claude_twist_sweep.py` (0/600 mismatches against
Magma; see `notes/claude_top10_07_2216.md`) — port it rather than
re-deriving. (iii) If every variant is insoluble at small height, run
`local-obstructions`-style checks on the condition conic before concluding
thinness.
