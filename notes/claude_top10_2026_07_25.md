### 1. Contact-7 SPLITALL — `[2,2,2,14]`, order 112, a new record — plus an infinite generic `[2,2,14]` family and `[2,2,28]`

**NEW (this review).**  The contact-7 chart's entire rational-root structure
is governed by three *universal linear relations*, which I derived and verified
symbolically.  With

```text
h = 1 - (7/2)x + a x^2 + b x^3 ,      f = (h^2 + (x-1)^7)/x^2      (monic quintic)
```

a rational root `r` of `f` forces `r = 1 - v^2` with `v` rational, and the five
`v_i` are exactly the roots of

```text
Q(v) = (v+1)^2 (c4 v^2 + c0) + v^5 - v^3 - v^2/2 ,     c4 = b+2,  c0 = 5/2-(a+b)
     = v^5 + c4 v^4 + (2c4-1) v^3 + (c4+c0-1/2) v^2 + 2 c0 v + c0 .
```

Because only two parameters enter, the elementary symmetric functions of the
five roots satisfy, **identically in `(a,b)`**,

```text
(R1)  e2 + 2 e1 + 1 = 0
(R2)  e4 + 2 e5     = 0            (equivalently  sum 1/v_i = -2)
(R3)  2 e3 - 2 e1 - 2 e5 - 1 = 0
```

(verified in `code/claude_c7_relations.gp`).  Equivalently: the problem is
"find a line meeting the rational plane quintic `X = v^2, Y = G(v)` in `k`
rational points", where `G(v) = -(v^5-v^3-v^2/2)/(v+1)^2`.

The rung structure is then completely explicit, and it is the *same ladder
that produced Elkies' order-80 record on contact-5*:

| rational `v_i` | quintic factor type | 2-rank | torsion | status |
|---:|:---|---:|:---|:---|
| 1 | `[1,4]` | 1 | `[14]` | realized |
| 2 | `[1,1,3]` | 2 | `[2,14]` | realized (rational surface) |
| 3 | `[1,1,1,2]` | 3 | `[2,2,14]` | **realized 2026-07-23, five End=Z curves** |
| 5 | `[1,1,1,1,1]` | 4 | **`[2,2,2,14]`, order 112** | never derived, never searched |

Four rational roots force the fifth (the quintic is monic), so the order-112
locus is the double cover of the three-root surface `R(s,t,u)=0` given by
"the residual quadratic splits".  The repo names this only as a one-line
"bonus watch"; no derivation and no search exist.

**What I did today.**  I wrote a two-tier `C` harvester

builds the residual cubic modulo small primes with `O(1)` has-a-root bitmaps,

### 2. Generic `[2,22]`: route B2′ on a directly parametrized `[1,1,2,2]` ansatz, and route B4 (Galois-stable Richelot)

The last `^RM` flag in the paper.  The 2026-07-23/24 work closed the obvious
doors properly: every parametrized order-11 family is 2-upgrade-locked (three
independent rank-0 kills over Flynn, two genus-3 blockers pointless to
parameter height `10^6`, and DS-v2 has no low-genus strata at all), and the
extended-DB 11-slice was counted exhaustively (`[2,22] x 1`).  Two live
routes remain, plus one that has never been run.

**B2′ — the BLP `[1,1,2,2]` locus, made directly searchable.**  BLP2009's
ansatz is `y^2 = R(x)^2 - 4c^2 S(x)^2` with `R = x^3-x^2+ax+b`, `S = x^2+d`,
i.e. `f = (R-2cS)(R+2cS)`, a product of two cubics.  The `[1,1,2,2]` shape
(2-rank 2, the shape both known `[2,22]` curves have) is exactly "each cubic
acquires a rational root", and **that condition is linear**: given
`(c, d, r1, r2)`, the two equations `(R-2cS)(r1) = 0` and `(R+2cS)(r2) = 0`
solve for `(a,b)`.  So the 2-structure is free, and only `ord(D_inf) = 11`
has to be imposed — and *that* is testable in microseconds by the polynomial
continued fraction machinery the repo already has and has validated
(`pell-cf-order`, `code/agent_a2_24_cf.m`).  The route has two rational
anchors: BLP's corrected row C4 (`r1 = 9, r2 = -21`) and, after
normalization, `19044.h.2`.  **No code has ever been written for this.**
This is the concrete form of "build the 2-structure in from the start", the
project's recorded winning pattern.

**B4 — Richelot from generic `[22]` streams.  NEW (this review): the
recorded blocker is wrong in general.**  Commit `34cfb59` blocks Richelot
walks because "`[22]`/`[14]` seeds are 2-rank 1, so they have no rational
`(2,2)`-kernels".  A Richelot kernel need only be **Galois-stable**, not
pointwise rational.  I verified in Magma that a curve with
`TwoTorsionSubgroup = [2]` can have a Richelot isogeny over `Q`
(`y^2 = (x^2+x+1)(x^4-4x^2+2)`, one neighbour), and measured the mechanism on
a 121-curve sample of 2-rank-1 curves whose quartic cofactor has Galois group
inside `D4`: **88 neighbours, of which 1 had higher 2-rank** — so roughly one
in ninety neighbours raises the 2-rank.  Since Flynn's `(6,2)` component
supplies *infinitely many* `[2,4]`-type members (its double cover is the
rank-1 conductor-92 curve `y^2 = x^3+3x^2+2x+1`), the recipe is: parametrize
enough members, keep those whose quartic cofactor has a rational resolvent-
cubic root (Galois `⊆ D4`), Richelot-map each over `Q`, exact-test.  A
`(2,2)`-isogeny preserves `End^0` and the odd part, so genericity and the 11
survive.  ~100 usable seeds are needed; the first computation is the
genus/rank of the resolvent-cubic cover over the `(6,2)` component.

**Two corrections that matter here.**

* Richelot-walking the *RM* anchors (`19044.h.2`, BLP C4corr) cannot produce
  a generic witness — `End^0` is an isogeny invariant, so every neighbour is
  again `RM(sqrt5)`.  The technique audit suggests doing this; for the
  generic goal it is a dead end (it is still worth doing for new `[2,22]`
  *examples*).
* **The two Magma jobs that constitute route B1 have been hung for 34 hours.**
  PID 48793 (`code/claude_v7_cfbackward.m`, last log line 19 minutes in) and
  PID 286896 (`code/claude_v7_stratum2b.m`, last log line *two seconds* in)
  are at ~99% CPU with no output, both with `SetMemoryLimit(24*10^9)` against
  the lab's 3 GB convention, and they occupy 2 of the 3 permitted Magma
  slots.  Kill them by PID (and sweep for detached `magma.exe` children), and

### 4. Run the database queries that were never run (cheapest decisive item on the board)

**The extended-database group list is inherited, not measured.**  The
2026-07-22 census only *refined witnesses for groups already on the
collaborators' hand-curated `NotesAndTodo.tex` Table 1* (`torsion_subgroup IN
(...)`, batched ≤14 labels).  No torsion-group histogram over the 6.2M-curve
Booker–Sutherland collection exists anywhere in the repo, so "absent from the
database" is an assumption for **every open target except the 11-slice**.

Four queries, minutes of work:

```sql
SELECT torsion_subgroup, count(*) FROM g2c_curves_new
  WHERE is_simple_geom GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 2 DESC;                       -- the histogram
SELECT torsion_subgroup, torsion_order, label, cond, geom_end_alg FROM g2c_curves_new
  WHERE is_simple_geom ORDER BY torsion_order DESC LIMIT 50;             -- max torsion
SELECT geom_end_alg, torsion_subgroup, count(*) FROM g2c_curves_new
  WHERE is_simple_geom AND geom_end_alg <> 'Q' GROUP BY 1,2;             -- RM/CM/QM seeds
SELECT class, array_agg(DISTINCT torsion_subgroup), max(torsion_order) FROM g2c_curves_new
  GROUP BY class HAVING count(DISTINCT torsion_subgroup) > 1;            -- isogeny jumps
```

Two supporting facts.  (i) A "DB says RM-only" verdict was **overturned within
a week** for `[2,2,14]` — small conductor is populated by Eisenstein/modular
factories that *force* RM (both RM witnesses have square conductor,
`138^2` and `390^2`), so DB endomorphism statistics are weak evidence about
the generic world; read `[2,22]` the same way.  (ii) Conductor is an isogeny
invariant, so if the extended DB is conductor-complete then *no* 2-power
isogeny walk from a DB seed can produce a group absent from the histogram —
which retro-explains all eleven zero-hit Richelot campaigns and makes the
histogram strictly more valuable than any further walking.

Also restore the lost Sutherland 5-smooth screening pipeline (487,493 curves,

### 8. `[3,12]`, order 36 — the smallest open group

### 9. Beyond order 96: `[2,2,2,24]`/`[2,2,4,12]` (192), `[2,2,4,8]` (128), and the multigrade infinitude question

The record component is understood: that component of `M(2,2,2,12)` **is** the
classical multigrade variety

```text
X1^2+X2^2+X3^2 = B1^2+B2^2 ,      X1^4+X2^4+X3^4 = B1^4+B2^4
```

on which both square conditions hold identically.  Exactly three curves are
known and the orbit census is complete to `X1,X2 <= 250,000`, `X3 <= 10^7`.
Three concrete items:

* **The literature dig on classical parametric multigrade families** (Gloden;
  Choudhry, *Symmetric Diophantine systems*) for the mixed-size `(3,2)` system
  of degrees `(2,4)`.  A single nondegenerate parametric family gives
  **infinitely many order-96 curves**, which is what every order-192 route
  needs (three lottery tickets is not a program).  Nobody has looked.
* **The ten pair-type components** of `M(2,2,2,12)` (halving
  `[(-e,0)+(-e',0)-2inf]`) are recorded as completely unexplored; only the
  five point-type criteria were scanned, to `(s:m:n)` height 60.
* `[2,2,2,24]`: of 52 repeated square-ratio fiber families from a complete
  `B=10000` transverse box, 41 are killed by complete one-prime obstructions
  plus separate closures of `W1`, `V2` and the `U2` infinity chart, leaving
  **eleven** locally-viable families (`F1 F6 N3 N4 N11 O4 O9 R3 R7 S3 W2`),
  none of which has had the component-complete treatment that closed the
  others.  That protocol is written and reusable.

For `[2,2,4,8]` (order 128) the only credible remaining route is the
positive-rank elliptic surface through the HLP point in the `(rho,sigma,tau)`
chart (`rho0 = 58466134224/53109477625`); the sign-reduction theorem makes the
`tor2244`-side deep search past `d = 65535` the only other option on the
all-squares model.

**One correction that changes the picture here.  NEW (this review, confirming
an audit finding):** the `[2,2,2,12]` quintic-chart enumerator's

## 3.  Cross-cutting method notes

1. **Galois-stable beats rationally-split.**  Several lanes have searched for
   *fully rational* factorizations where only a *Galois-stable partition* is
   needed — most importantly the Richelot kernels above.  The same relaxation
   is worth auditing wherever the repo requires "the quartic splits into two
   rational quadratics".
2. **Small-prime walls on contact charts are usually chart artifacts.**  The
   sharpest recorded instance: `[35]` dies at `p=3` on contact-7+5 and at
   `p=3,5` on contact-5+7, yet on Elkies' full `A_1(5)` threefold `5 | #J`
   holds at every smooth chart point at every prime and `35 | #J` has density
   `~1/7`.  Working on the full moduli threefold instead of a contact slice
   produced a new infinite exact-`[15]` family and a second infinite exact-
   `[30]` family.
3. **Prefilters that are too aggressive hide the answer.**  The old `(2,30)`
   level-B sieve demanded 2-rank `>= 2` at every prime and found nothing; a
   3-only sieve instantly found 76 survivors at height 60 and led to two new
   infinite certified-simple families.
4. **A failed kill is often the most valuable result.**  The refuted rank-0
   kill for the `(2,2,2,12)` near-miss locus — the discovery that the
   conditions were algebraically correlated — is what produced the order-96
   record two days later.
5. **Certificates: scan the prime, and require root-power strictness at
   both.**  `p=13` fails for `(29,121,125,145)` (works at 61); `p=11` fails
   for Nicholls' `[30]` (works at 13); `p=5` is biquadratic for the `[28]`
   curve so the test cannot even run.  An even `chi` at the *second* prime
   (e.g. `x^4-2x^2+1681`) leaves an `M_2`-type geometric reduction where RM
   can hide — this repaired three pre-existing certificates.
