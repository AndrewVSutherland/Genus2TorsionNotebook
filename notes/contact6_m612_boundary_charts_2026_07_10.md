# Contact-6 `[6,12]`: the `5`- and `7`-boundary charts

Date: 2026-07-10.

## Main conclusions

1. The quantity called `RR` in the first route note is not a new boundary
   curve.  Exactly

   ```text
   RR = ((a+3)(b+3)-4)^2
        -(b^2-2a-3)(a^2-2b-3)
      = Res(B,C)
      = 2(a+b+2)^3.
   ```

   Thus its reduced support is the rational line `a+b+2=0`.

2. Every reduced component of the **base** boundary is rational.  In
   `[A:B:T]=[a:b:1]` the complete support is

   ```text
   B+3T,
   A+3T,
   A+B+2T,
   DBh=A^2-6AT-8BT-15T^2,
   DCh=B^2-6BT-8AT-15T^2,
   T.
   ```

   The two conics are parabolas with rational parametrizations given below.

3. On every one of the five affine base-boundary supports modulo `5`, the
   full cubic-contact equations with `M=L^2` have **no `F_5` point** satisfying

   ```text
   L*v*(U^2-4v^2) != 0.
   ```

   This is stronger than the old statement that the good curve chart has no
   `[6,12]` point.  If `a,b,L,U,v` are all `5`-integral, a rational core point
   on an affine bad-reduction residue must simultaneously enter at least one
   of the deeper core boundaries

   ```text
   L=0,  v=0,  U=2v,  U=-2v                 (mod 5).
   ```

   Poles in `(L,U,v)` give additional weighted-infinity charts and are not
   ruled out by this computation.

4. Modulo `7`, all five affine supports do have smooth, nondegenerate
   cubic-contact points with a first-order transverse lift.  Hence `p=7`
   does not kill the affine boundary route by itself.  The viable base
   parameters are very concentrated and are listed below.

5. At base-projective infinity, the ordinary chart is highly non-flat but
   its first two layers can be written exactly.  Away from the special point
   `[0:1:0]`, every nondegenerate first-order branch is forced onto
   `v^3=1`.  Over `Q` this is the rational special branch `v=1`.  The one
   infinity point that avoids this first-order condition is

   ```text
   [A:B:T]=[0:1:0],
   ```

   which is also the intersection of infinity with `a+3=0` and the tangent
   point of `DB=0`.  It is the most interesting ordinary infinity chart for
   the next blow-up.


## Exact base geometry

Recall

```text
B=(b+3)x^2+(a-3)x+2,
C=2x^2+(b-3)x+(a+3),
f=x*B*C,
DB=(a-3)^2-8(b+3),
DC=(b-3)^2-8(a+3).
```

Direct symbolic factorization gives

```text
Disc_x(f) = 16*(a+3)^2*(a+b+2)^6*DB*DC.
```

The degree-loss divisor `b+3=0` is absent from this specialized degree-5
discriminant polynomial and must be adjoined separately.

The six projective supports have the following parametrizations:

```text
b+3:       [u:-3v:v]
a+3:       [-3v:u:v]
a+b+2:     [u:-u-2v:v]
DB:        [8uv:u^2-6uv-15v^2:8v^2]
DC:        [u^2-6uv-15v^2:8uv:8v^2]
infinity:  [u:v:0].
```

At infinity there are three distinguished intersection points:

```text
[1:0:0]    lies on b+3 and is the tangent point of DC,
[0:1:0]    lies on a+3 and is the tangent point of DB,
[1:-1:0]   lies on a+b+2.
```

The complete pointwise stratification is generated by
`code/contact6_m612_boundary_base.m`.  The boundary union contains `21` of
the `31` points of `P^2(F_5)` and `33` of the `57` points of `P^2(F_7)`.
In particular, the apparent `RR` stratum is exactly the `a+b+2` stratum in
both characteristics.


## Local affine core charts

For each reduced support use a transverse parameter `e`:

```text
b+3:    a=t,                    b=-3+e,
a+3:    a=-3+e,                 b=t,
RRred:  a=t,                    b=-t-2+e,
DB:     a=t,                    b=(t-3)^2/8-3+e,
DC:     b=t,                    a=(t-3)^2/8-3+e.
```

The last two formulas have `DB=-8e` and `DC=-8e`, respectively, so they
are genuine transverse coordinates at `p=5,7`.

Write the three cubic-contact equations as `F=(F1,F2,F3)` after imposing
`M=L^2`.  A raw substitution `e=0` is misleading: the singular curve fiber
acquires excess-dimensional contact components.  A cheap exact necessary
test for a branch transverse to the divisor is instead

```text
(dF/d(t,L,U,v))(z0) * z1 = -(dF/de)(z0).
```

The code enumerates every raw point and solves this linear system exactly.
The results are:

Several are boundary intersections rather than generic points of one

There are also pure-component residues, for example `(0,4)` on `b+3`,
`(4,0)` on `a+3`, `(5,1)` on `DB`, and `(1,5)` on `DC`.

Saturating the raw `p=5` fibers by `L*v*(U^2-4v^2)` shows that the
contact-open boundary schemes for `b+3`, `a+3`, and `a+b+2` are
one-dimensional over the algebraic closure even though they have no
`F_5`-points.  Thus the `p=5` result is a rational-residue obstruction, not
geometric emptiness.  Full flat-closure Groebner saturation was prepared in

comfortable memory envelope tonight, so the first-order enumeration is the

## Projective infinity

Use `[A:B:T]=[t:1:e]`, so `a=t/e`, `b=1/e`.  The base degrees of
`(F1,F2,F3)` are `(2,4,3)`.  With `M=L^2`, their leading forms at `e=0` are

```text
F1_inf =  4*M*(1-M)*v^3,
F2_inf = 16*M^2*(1-M)^2,
F3_inf =  8*M^2*(1-M).
```

Consequently a contact-open point (`L != 0`) has

```text
M=1,  hence L=+/-1.
```

At `L=1` (and identically at `L=-1`) the first-order middle equation
vanishes, while eliminating the one first-order `L` correction between the
other two equations gives

```text
t*(v^3-1)=0.
```

Thus a generic infinity point has `v^3=1`; the exception `t=0` is
`[0:1:0]`.

In the other chart `[A:B:T]=[1:t:e]`, the overlap condition is

```text
t^3*(v^3-1)=0.
```

At its exceptional endpoint `t=0`, namely `[1:0:0]`, the leading forms all
vanish.  Direct first-order expansion gives only

```text
L^2*(v^3-1)=0.
```

So on the contact-open endpoint one again has `v^3=1`, but `L` is not yet
forced to `+/-1`.  These identities are characteristic-zero calculations,
not fits to finite data; they are checked by
`code/contact6_m612_boundary_infinity_leading.m`.

The ordinary infinity closure is still very singular: its fiber Jacobian
has rank at most `1` at every enumerated `p=5,7` point.  Higher-order or
weighted charts are therefore essential before treating any of these as a
genuine component of the core.
