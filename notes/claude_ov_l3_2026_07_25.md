## 1. The Humbert-5 equation

`code/claude_ov_l3_humbert5.m`, log `results/claude_ov_l3_humbert5.log`,
equation `results/claude_ov_l3_humbert5_eqn.txt`.

Source of the parametrisation: the Elkies–Kumar birational model of the
Hilbert modular surface `Y_-(5)`, as quoted in (2.1) of K. Martin, *Moduli for
rational genus 2 curves with real multiplication for discriminant 5*
(arXiv:2206.05752, JTNB 36 (2024)):

```text
(I2 : I4 : I6 : I10) = ( 24g + 6 : 9g^2 : 81g^3 + 18g^2 + 36h : 4h^2 ).
```

A point of `A_2` lies on `H_5` iff its Igusa–Clebsch invariants agree with some
`(g,h)` after the weighted scaling `(I2,I4,I6,I10) -> (u I2, u^2 I4, u^3 I6,
u^5 I10)`.  Eliminating `g` and `h` gives two conditions on `u`

```text
A(u) = (u J2 - 6)^2 - 64 u^2 J4                       (deg 2 in u)
B(u) = 36^2 * ( 4 h(u)^2 - u^5 J10 ),  h = (u^3 J6 - 81 g^3 - 18 g^2)/36
                                                      (deg 6 in u)
```

and `H5 = Res_u(A,B)` is **irreducible over `Q`, 16 terms, weighted degree 24**
(weights `I2,I4,I6,I10 = 2,4,6,10`):

```text
H5 = J2^4 J4^4 - 12 J2^3 J4^3 J6 - 972 J2^3 J4^2 J10 - 2 J2^2 J4^5
   + 54 J2^2 J4^2 J6^2 + 5832 J2^2 J4 J6 J10 + 236196 J2^2 J10^2
   + 12 J2 J4^4 J6 - 16524 J2 J4^3 J10 - 108 J2 J4 J6^3 - 8748 J2 J6^2 J10
   + J4^6 - 18 J4^3 J6^2 + 46656 J4^2 J6 J10 - 15116544 J4 J10^2 + 81 J6^4
```

`Z(H5)` is a hypersurface in the weighted `P(2,4,6,10)`, i.e. 2-dimensional,
and it contains the 2-dimensional image of the parametrisation, so as a set
`Z(H5) = H_5` **provided `H5` is absolutely irreducible** — otherwise `Z(H5)`
could be `H_5` together with Galois-conjugate extra sheets, and every test
below would be measuring the wrong surface.

`code/claude_ov_l3_h5irred.m` (log `results/claude_ov_l3_h5irred.log`) settles
it.  `H5` is irreducible over `Q` (one factor, multiplicity 1, total degree 8);
`H5 mod 7` has no degree drop and is irreducible over `F_{7^k}` for
`k = 1,…,8`.  Since `H5` irreducible over `F_p` with `r` absolute factors has
Frobenius acting transitively on them, each absolute factor is defined over
`F_{p^r}` with `r ≤ deg H5 = 8`; so `H5 mod 7` is absolutely irreducible, hence
so is `H5` over `Q`.  Therefore

```text
Z(H5) = H_5      exactly (as sets, over \bar Q).
```

## 3. THE ANOMALY: the real-subfield-disc RM screen is broken

`code/claude_ov_l3_h5validate.m` (predecessor, 1000 LMFDB `RM` curves) produced

H5 ≠ 0  & census = {d}, d ≠ 5   254
H5 ≠ 0  & census scatters       371      <-- ditto
                                ----

`code/claude_ov_l3_h5diag.m` prints the per-prime detail and the cause is
immediate: **at every offending prime, `a1 = 0` exactly.**  Example
(`21168.b3`, LMFDB `geom_end_alg = RM`, `is_gl2_type = false`):

SUMMARY 21168.b3 : primes=43 core5=23 other=20 (19 of the 20 have a1 = 0;
                   the 1 remaining has n = 0)

### Mechanism

If `A/Q` has geometric RM by `K` that is **not defined over `Q`** — the RM is
defined over a quadratic field `L`, LMFDB's `is_gl2_type = false` — then Galois
acts on `End(A_{\bar Q}) ⊗ Q = K` through `Gal(L/Q)` by the nontrivial
automorphism.  At a prime `p` **inert** in `L` the extra twist forces
`a_1 = Tr(a_p) = 0` exactly.  Then

```text
n_p = a1^2 - 4(a2 - 2p) = 8p - 4 a2
```

and `Q(√n_p)` is *not* the RM field — it is junk that varies with `p`.  Inert
primes have density 1/2, so such a curve looks exactly like `End = Z` under the
naive screen.

The counting confirms it: of the **4990** LMFDB `geom_end_alg = 'RM'` curves,
**3116 = 62.4%** have `is_gl2_type = false`; the predecessor's 1000-row sample
scattered on **613 = 61.3%**.

### The repair (one line)

> A prime is informative for the census **only if `a1 ≠ 0`.**  (Also discard
> `n_p = 0` and `n_p` a perfect square — those are the primes where `J_{F_p}`
> is isogenous to `E^2` or splits.)

Restricted to those primes the census is again constant and equal to the true
RM discriminant core.  Measured in `claude_ov_l3_h5gold.m` on the 116
gold-standard curves and in `claude_ov_l3_h5validate2.m` on
4990 RM + 2882 `End = Z` curves.

## 4. THE MAIN QUESTION: is `Σ'' ⊆ H_5`?

`Σ''` = `{ ord(D_inf) = 11 }` inside the monic `[1,1,2,2]` chart

```text
y^2 = x (x-1) (x^2 + p1 x + p2) (x^2 + p3 x + p4).
```

`Σ''` is an honest algebraic surface in `A^4`: it is `s^{-1}(0)` for the
section `s = [11·D_inf]` of the relative Jacobian over the nondegenerate locus,
so its formation commutes with base change and `Σ''(F_p)` is exactly the set of
`(p1,p2,p3,p4) ∈ F_p^4` with `ord(D_inf) | 11` in `J(F_p)`.  The pullback of
`H_5` to the chart is a hypersurface (a 3-fold).  Hence:

* if `Σ'' ⊆ H_5~` then **every** point of `Σ''(F_p)` satisfies `H5 = 0`;
* if not, `Σ'' ∩ H_5~` is at most a curve, and only `O(p)` of the `≈ c p^2`
  points satisfy it.

`code/claude_ov_b2p_scan.c surface P dump` enumerates `Σ''(F_p)` exhaustively
over `F_p^4` (order **exactly** 11, so the degenerate `D_inf = 0` locus is
excluded); `code/claude_ov_l3_humbtest.m` evaluates `H5` at every point.

**Answer: NO.  `Σ''` is not contained in `H_5`.**

* the **off**-`H_5` part is `≈ 8 p^2` at all four primes — genuinely
  2-dimensional, i.e. a positive proportion (95.5% at `p = 199`) of `Σ''`;
* the **on**-`H_5` part has `frac · p ≈ 8` — a constant, exactly the signature
  of a proper (codimension-1) intersection, the same signature the ambient
  calibration gives with constant `≈ 2`.  It is a **curve**, not a component:
  `onH5 / p^2` = 0.50, 0.74, 0.40, 0.39, 0.35 is `< 1` at every prime, and an
  absolutely irreducible surface component rational over `F_p` would
  contribute `p^2 + O(p^{3/2})` on its own.

### Why the mod-`p` test is not merely suggestive here

`Σ'' = s^{-1}(0)` for a section `s` of an abelian scheme of relative dimension
2, so by Krull every irreducible component of `Σ''` over `Z[1/2]` has
codimension `≤ 2` in the 5-dimensional `A^4_{Z}`, i.e. dimension `≥ 3`.  A
*vertical* component (one living only in the fibre over a single `p`) would
therefore be a 3-fold inside `A^4_{F_p}` and would contribute `≳ p^3` points.
We count `9.05 p^2 = 0.036 p^3` at `p = 251`.  So there are **no vertical
components at `p = 61, 101, 151, 199, 251`**, hence `Σ''(F_p)` is exactly the
reduction of the `Z`-closure of `Σ''_Q` at those primes.  If `Σ''_Q ⊆ H_5` then
that reduction would satisfy `H5 ≡ 0`; 548 096 of 570 428 points do not.

(The C sieve's `nondegenerate()` test rejects exactly the degenerate `f`: both
quadratics separable, neither vanishing at 0 or 1, and `Res(q_1,q_2) ≠ 0`.  So
every point counted lies in the open locus `U` where the relative Jacobian is
an abelian scheme and `s` is defined, which is what the dimension argument
needs.)

### 4.3 Is `Σ''` inside some *other* Humbert surface?

Refuting `H_5` leaves the loophole "maybe `Σ''` is RM-forced by a different
discriminant".  Deriving `H_Δ` for each `Δ` needs an Elkies–Kumar
parametrisation we do not have, so test it **intrinsically** instead
(`code/claude_ov_l3_rmspectrum.m`, `code/claude_ov_l3_rmspec_ctrl.m`):

> For a point of `Σ''(F_p)` the real Weil subfield of `Q(π)` is `Q(√n)`,
> `n = a1^2 - 4(a2 - 2p)`.  A whole *component* with RM by `Δ` is `≈ 1/8.35` of
> `Σ''(F_p)`, so it would show up as a ≈12-percentage-point **spike** at
> `core(Δ)` in the histogram of squarefree cores.

The naive ambient histogram is the *wrong* control, because every point of
`Σ''` has `11 | #J(F_p)`, which by itself biases `(a1,a2)`.  The right control
is random chart points **conditioned on `11 | #J(F_p)`**; that is what
`claude_ov_l3_rmspec_ctrl.m` computes (24 000 probes at `p = 101`, of which
2 394 satisfy the condition).

inside `H_12` (RM by `Q(√3)`) would make `core = 3` occur at ≈100% on `1/8.3`

`p = 61` (observed 0.263 — compatible) but 0.21 at `p = 101` (observed 0.114 —

rational at both primes.  What two primes cannot exclude is a geometric

> **no component of `Σ''` outside `H_5` carries real multiplication of any

> surface: its generic point has `End = Z`.

Limitation: this test sees RM that is defined over `F_p`.  A component whose RM
was defined only over a quadratic extension would (by §3!) show the spike at
only half the primes — but at half strength that is still ≈6 points, far above
what we observe, so the conclusion is unaffected.

## 5. Conclusions

### 5.1 On the lane's question

> **`Σ'' ⊄ H_5`.**  The `[1,1,2,2] ∩ ord(D_inf)=11` locus is a surface of which
> only a *curve* lies on the Humbert surface of discriminant 5.  Both known
> `[2,22]` witnesses, and all 17 tuples of the `H = 60` sweep, lie on that
> curve.

So the "RM-forcing" explanation of `notes/claude_ov_b2p_result_2026_07_25.md`
— "the 2-rank-2 sublocus may be Humbert-forced even though the ambient BLP
stratum is not" — is **false**.  The constancy of the RM signature over the
`H = 60` sweep is not geometry; it is the arithmetic accident that the only
rational points of `Σ''` of height `≤ 60` happen to lie on the `H_5` curve.
Route B2' is **not** closed: `Σ''` is 2-dimensional, ≈95% of it is off `H_5`,
and a generic (`End = Z`) `[2,22]` curve, if one exists, lives there.

§4.3 closes the obvious loophole: the off-`H_5` part carries **no** RM of any
discriminant — its Frobenius real-subfield spectrum is that of a random chart
point with `11 | #J(F_p)`.  So `Σ''` is not inside any Humbert surface, and its
generic point has `End = Z`.

Two honest limits on this statement:

* the refutation is a mod-`p` argument at five primes, made rigorous by the
  Krull dimension bound above, not a symbolic elimination;
* §4.3's spectrum test is statistical (`p = 101` only, 8 523 sampled points
  against a 2 394-point control) and would not detect an RM component
  contributing less than ≈3% of `Σ''(F_p)`.

### 5.2 On the screen

> The Frobenius **real-subfield-disc census is not a valid `End = Z` test** as
> it has been used in this repository.  It must be restricted to primes with
> `a1 ≠ 0`.

Unrepaired, it declares `End = Z` for 62% of all curves with geometric RM.
Repaired, it agrees with LMFDB's certified endomorphism data on the
gold-standard set and returns a single discriminant.  Every past "scattering,
therefore `End = Z`" verdict in the repo should be re-run in the repaired form;
verdicts backed by the strict two-prime Frobenius certificate are unaffected.

## 6. What was NOT checked

* **The Elkies–Kumar parametrisation was taken on trust** from Martin
  (2.1)/arXiv:2206.05752 — not re-derived.  It is checked against that paper's
  explicit RM-5 curve (all four invariants match after one scaling) and then
  against 116 LMFDB curves, so an error in it would have to be a very lucky
  one, but the formula itself is quoted, not proved.
* **The containment refutation is mod-`p`, at 5 primes.**  It is not a symbolic
  elimination.  §4's dimension argument closes the vertical-component loophole
  at those five primes, but the statement "`Σ''_Q ⊄ H_5`" rests on that
  argument plus the correctness of `cford_fp` in the C sieve (which is
  validated only by its two positive controls and by the `H = 60` sweep
  reproducing both witnesses end to end).
* **No explicit algebraic point of `Σ''` off `H_5` was produced.**  Hensel-
  lifting one of the 548 096 off-`H_5` points at `p = 251` to `Z_p` would turn
  the mod-`p` argument into an unconditional one; that needs Jacobian
  arithmetic over `Z/p^k`, which was not attempted.
* **No new rational `[2,22]` curve was searched for.**  The `H = 60` sweep of
  `notes/claude_ov_b2p_result_2026_07_25.md` was *not* extended: on 192 cores it
  cost 6.7 h, and `H = 100` is 7.4× larger, which on claude-box's 32 shared
  vCPUs is ≈ 300 h.  Nothing was learned about heights above 60.
* **The `Σ'' ∩ H_5` curve was not identified.**  We know a curve's worth of
  `Σ''` lies in `H_5` and that both witnesses are on it; its equation, genus and
  rational points are unknown.  That is the natural next computation (§7).
* **The `core = 0` excess of §4.3 was not chased.**  `Σ''` off `H_5` meets the
  "`J_{F_p} ~ E^2`" locus 5× more often than the control.  We read it as a
  curve of intersection with the square/product locus; it could also be a
  small component, which would matter (such points are geometrically
  non-simple and useless for this project).  One prime, one sample.
* **The `core = 3` residual of §4.3 was not resolved.**  Two primes exclude an
  `H_12` component rational at both; they do not exclude one defined over a
  quadratic field.  The clean way to settle it is a third and fourth prime
  (`p = 151, 199`, dumps already on disk) plus a control that records `#J(F_p)`
  so the `11/#J` weight can be divided out.  That is the sharpest open
  sub-question this note leaves.
* **The spectrum test was run at `p = 61, 101` only**, and only for `Σ''` as a
  whole, not component by component.
* `code/claude_ov_l3_h5anomaly.m` (the predecessor's non-maximal-order
  detector, `D_p = 5 k_p^2` ⇒ conductor divides `gcd_p(k_p)`) was read and
  committed but **not run**: §2.2 answers its question directly from LMFDB's
  certified `ring_geom` index, which is better evidence than a Frobenius gcd.
